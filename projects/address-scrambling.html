Flex-VecPool: Smashing the Bandwidth Wall

SystemVerilog Computer Architecture Many-Core Optimization

The Setup: IIS @ ETH Zürich

This wasn't just homework; this was a Semester Project at the Integrated Systems Laboratory (IIS) at ETH Zürich, under the supervision of Prof. Luca Benini. The goal? Push the boundaries of many-core architectures. My work is now being integrated into a research paper where I'll be listed as a co-author.

The Problem: The Bandwidth Wall

Modern many-core SoCs are fast, but they have a drinking problem: they can't gulp down data fast enough. When hundreds of vector units try to access shared memory simultaneously, they hit a "bandwidth wall." The compute power is there, but the data isn't.

The Red Box: Flex-VecPool

Enter Flex-VecPool (the red box in the diagram). By fusing the run-time address-scrambling engine of Flex-TeraPool with the vectorized core clusters of MemPool-Spatz, I created a fully transparent Scratch-Pad Memory (SPM) that reconfigures itself on the fly. It's like rearranging the library while people are still reading books—to make sure everyone finds what they need instantly.

Big Gains, Tiny Cost

The hardware overhead for this magic? Less than 1% silicon area. But the payoff is massive:

  • 4.6x Faster Dot Product on TeraPool.
  • 3.0x Faster GEMV (General Matrix-Vector Multiplication).
  • Seamless Scaling from small MinPool clusters to massive TeraPool systems.
