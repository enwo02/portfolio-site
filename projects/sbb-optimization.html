Predictive rail switch heating

6-month internship at SBB (Swiss Federal Railways)

Python Physics Modeling Optimization Energy Efficiency

The Mission

Rail switches need to stay ice-free in winter, or trains can't switch tracks. Simple enough, right? The catch: traditional heating systems pre-heat the switches in the morning or heat to high tempertures, burning energy like there's no tomorrow—even when it's not necessary. The challenge? Cut the waste without freezing the switches.

The Solution: Physics Meets Prediction

I built a physics-based model that predicts rail temperatures a few hours ahead. Think of it as a weather forecast, but for metal tracks. The algorithm looks into the future, figures out exactly when heating is needed, and only fires up the system then. No more guesswork, no more wasted energy during those borderline "maybe it'll freeze?" moments.

The Results: Pilot Project Win

Does it work? Absolutely. We ran a side-by-side comparison against the traditional heating algorithm in a real-world pilot project, and the numbers speak for themselves:

  • 24% Energy Cost Savings: Compared to the old system. That's real money saved and a serious dent in carbon emissions.
  • 99.87% Reliability: The switches stayed operational when needed. No frozen tracks, no delays, no angry commuters.
  • Smart Load Management: The system heats when trains are using less power, smoothing out demand spikes and making the grid happier.

Collaboration & Real-World Implementation

This wasn't just a solo coding adventure. I worked closely with the team that would actually use this thing—making sure the UI made sense and the functionality covered everything they needed. No ivory tower solutions here.
